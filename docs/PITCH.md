# 60-second pitch and demo shot list

## The spoken script

Roughly 150 words — that's 60 seconds at a calm pace. Do not rush it. The
demo carries the weight; the words just frame it.

> **[0:00 — hook, look at the room]**
> Everyone in this room has had a landlord ask for a rent increase. And almost
> nobody knows that Dubai law caps it.
>
> **[0:10 — the problem]**
> Decree 43 of 2013 sets a hard ceiling based on how far your rent sits below
> the market. It's public, it's precise — and tenants just pay whatever they're
> asked, because reading the decree isn't realistic when you're stressed and the
> renewal is due.
>
> **[0:22 — start the demo, upload]**
> So: this is a real tenancy contract. I upload it, and watch what the agent
> actually does.
>
> **[0:30 — narrate the tools as they run]**
> It reads the contract. Finds the market benchmark. Applies the decree. Checks
> every clause against Dubai tenancy law. And drafts what to say.
>
> **[0:45 — the verdict lands]**
> The landlord asked for fifteen percent. The legal maximum here is **zero**.
> That's seventeen thousand five hundred dirhams they aren't entitled to.
>
> **[0:55 — the close]**
> Every legal claim cites its article. It's live now — anyone can open it.

## If you have 90 seconds instead of 60

Add this after the verdict, because it's the strongest technical point:

> The agent cannot invent a law. It cites provisions by ID, and the tool checks
> each one against verified statute text — an ID that doesn't exist gets
> rejected. And when it can't determine something, it says so instead of
> guessing. That mattered more than accuracy to us: a confident wrong answer
> about someone's housing is worse than no answer.

## Shot list for the recording

Record at 1280×720 or higher. Browser at ~90% zoom so the verdict card and the
step panels are both readable.

| # | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Landing page | Let the headline sit for two seconds. Don't scroll yet. |
| 2 | Pick sample 1 from "Try a sample" | Say "a real tenancy contract" — it's a realistic Ejari-format contract with synthetic parties. Don't claim it's a real person's. |
| 3 | Press **Replay a recorded audit** | See the note below on why replay, not live. |
| 4 | The five step panels opening and completing | This is the money shot. Let it play. Don't talk over the whole thing. |
| 5 | Expand ONE tool panel | `calculate_legal_max` is the best one — shows inputs, outputs, and the citation. |
| 6 | The verdict card | Pause here. Three numbers: their rent, what's demanded, the lawful ceiling. |
| 7 | Scroll to the talking points | Read one aloud. |
| 8 | The footer | The "guidance, not legal advice" line and the benchmark provenance. Ten seconds. It reads as competence, not as a disclaimer. |

## Use the replay button, not a live run

The free-tier quota is roughly 20 requests per model per day and one audit costs
about six. A rate limit mid-recording would cost you a take, and mid-*pitch*
would be worse.

The replay is a genuine recorded run — the same steps, the same numbers, and the
UI labels it as a replay. Nothing is simulated.

If you want to prove it's live, do **one** live run at the very start on a
freshly-loaded page, then use replays for everything else. Have the replay open
in a second tab as a fallback.

## Record a backup take

Do the whole thing twice. The second take is always calmer, and if the first has
a stumble you won't have to re-set the app state under time pressure.

## Things not to say

- Don't call the benchmark "official" or "RERA data". It's an indicative
  estimate, and the app says so. If a judge who knows the domain catches you
  overclaiming, you lose the credibility the honesty was buying.
- Don't say "legal advice". Say "guidance".
- Don't claim the samples are real contracts. They're synthetic, and saying so
  costs you nothing.
